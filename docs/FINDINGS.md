### 3. Pollution is highly autocorrelated day to day

Lag-1 autocorrelation of daily PM2.5 — correlating each day against the mart's own `LAG()` column — runs from **0.72** (**Chennai**, Eastern Coastal) to **0.94** (**Mumbai**, Western Coastal). Today's air is mostly yesterday's air: a single day's reading carries much less independent information than it appears to.

The spread across these cities is narrow (0.72 to 0.94), so on this window the data does not separate "accumulating" from "ventilated" airsheds — every city in the set shows strong day-to-day carryover. A longer window covering the winter inversion would be needed to test that distinction.

Day-over-day churn is the same story from the other side: mean absolute DoD change spans 15.7% to 31.5%. This is the practical case for putting a 7-day rolling average next to the daily value — the daily number moves on weather, the rolling one on conditions.

## How to read the anomaly flag

`is_statistical_anomaly` is true when a day's mean PM2.5 is more than 2 sigma above the mean of the **preceding** 30 days, and only once at least 14 of those days exist. Excluding the current day matters: a day bad enough to matter would otherwise pull up the very baseline it is compared against, and the flag would under-report exactly the episodes it exists to catch.

`is_cpcb_breach` and `is_who_breach` are plain threshold tests and stay in the mart alongside it. The two answer different questions: the threshold says *is this air legal to breathe*, the Z-score says *is today unusual for this city*.
